Space begins about a hundred kilometres up.

KÁRMÁN carries eight travellers past that mark at once. Eleven minutes out to the black and home, five days orbiting the planet, or a week around the Moon's far side.

Nine engines, then a stillness nobody predicts.

02 · 94 KM · T+00:02:26

Stars, at eight o'clock in the morning.

02 · THE LINE · 100 KM

Beyond here, a wing has nothing left to push against.

Everything past this point is orbit, or a long fall. There is no wall, no jolt to mark it. The commander reads the number once, low, and the cabin quiets.

02 · SEPARATION · T+00:02:51

03 · ORBIT · 540 KM

Nothing holds you up here. You are falling around the planet.

At 7.6 kilometres a second, the ground drops away beneath you exactly as fast as you drop toward it. It looks and feels like floating. It is precisely the reverse.

03 · NIGHT PASS · T+0D 06:40

By night the coastlines glow in strings of sodium light.

Cities outline whole continents. Storms flash over the ocean as if someone were photographing them from below. Nobody sleeps through their first pass in darkness.

03 · SUNRISE · EVERY 92 MINUTES

Sixteen sunrises daily. Each lasts eight seconds flat.

A thin blue band, a red edge, then the sun all at once, with no atmosphere left to blur it. Ring voyagers see eighty of these. Most give up counting near twelve.

THE CABIN

We built the cabin around the window, then the window around you.

01 · SEAT

Moulded to your frame.

Your seat liner comes from a scan taken on your first day of training. It carries you through 5.4 g and travels home with you after.

02 · GLASS

Fused silica, 42 centimetres thick.

Three panes, the inner one heated, mounted close enough to your face that the frame drops out of view. Every voyager gets their own.

03 · LIGHT

Dim enough to see by.

Cabin lighting falls to 2 lux on the night side, warm and low near the floor, so your eyes adjust to both the stars and the cities beneath them.

04 · AIR

Sea-level, at 21°C.

Sea-level pressure, 40% humidity, filtered and cycled every ninety seconds. Tethys stocks twelve days of it for a seven-day trip.

THE TRAINING PROGRAMME

Six days to turn into
crew.

Ring voyagers train for six days at Solano Coast. Brink uses only three of them, and Tethys adds fifteen more spread across two months. You leave able to handle everything in the cabin you will almost certainly never need.

04 · TRANS-LUNAR INJECTION

A five-minute-fifty burn, and Earth's pull releases you.

Tethys departs orbit at 39,000 km/h. Within the hour, the planet you have spent your whole life on shrinks into the window frame.

04 · COAST · T+1D 20H

Two days out, the entire Earth hides behind your thumb.

You watch weather systems spin, the day-night line sweep an ocean, both poles glowing white at once. Everything anyone has ever done sits inside that small circle.

04 · FAR SIDE · LOSS OF SIGNAL

Behind the Moon, the radio falls silent for forty-one minutes.

No Earth, no ground crew on the line. Only the far side sliding past 8,200 kilometres below, terrain fewer than forty people have ever seen with their own eyes.

04 · ACQUISITION OF SIGNAL · 384,400 KM

Then it climbs.

Earthrise, above a grey horizon, in complete silence. Then a voice from Solano Coast, delayed four light-seconds, asking how the view looks.
